Abstract and Project Summary

Liver cirrhosis is a serious and progressive disease that significantly affects liver function and overall patient health. Accurate staging is important for treatment planning, prognosis evaluation, and long-term patient monitoring. However, liver disease classification is challenging because many clinical indicators overlap between stages.

In this project, multiple machine learning approaches were explored for multiclass liver disease staging using both structured clinical datasets and medical imaging datasets. Decision Trees, Random Forests, Multilayer Perceptrons (MLPs), and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) were implemented and evaluated in MATLAB. The project compared classification performance, interpretability, feature importance, and the ability of different models to capture nonlinear relationships within clinical and image-based data.

Introduction and Motivation

Liver cirrhosis is a chronic and progressive disease in which healthy liver tissue is replaced by scar tissue, leading to impaired liver function and potentially severe complications. Accurate staging of cirrhosis is important for treatment planning, disease monitoring, and long-term patient care, but distinguishing between stages can be difficult due to overlapping clinical indicators. Traditional diagnosis often depends on combinations of laboratory testing, medical imaging interpretation, and physician judgment.

This project explores the use of machine learning techniques to classify liver cirrhosis severity using structured clinical datasets and medical imaging data. Multiple approaches were investigated, including Decision Trees, Random Forests, Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) neural networks, and preliminary convolutional neural network (CNN) models for ultrasound image analysis. Machine learning provides an opportunity to analyze multiple clinical variables simultaneously and identify complex relationships within patient data that may not be obvious through conventional analysis methods.

Medical illustration related to liver disease progression and cirrhosis.

The goal of this work was to evaluate how effectively machine learning models can predict liver cirrhosis stage while exploring how structured clinical records and imaging-based workflows can support more integrated liver disease assessment systems. Previous studies have demonstrated strong potential for machine learning approaches in liver disease prediction, and this project expands upon those efforts by investigating multiple classification models alongside preliminary medical imaging analysis.

Project Scope

This project explored liver cirrhosis stage classification using both structured clinical datasets and liver ultrasound imaging data. Two related machine learning workflows were investigated.

  • Tabular Clinical Data Models: Decision Tree, Random Forest, and Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) models were evaluated using laboratory measurements, demographic information, and physician-observed clinical indicators associated with liver disease progression.

  • Medical Imaging Models: Convolutional neural network (CNN) approaches were explored using liver ultrasound image datasets in order to investigate imaging-based liver disease classification directly from medical scans.

  • Integrated Clinical Perspective: Although implemented separately, the two workflows represent closely connected parts of the same clinical assessment process. Many variables within the structured dataset, including ascites, hepatomegaly, edema, and spiders, are often derived from physician interpretation of imaging studies or physical examination findings before being entered numerically into patient records.

General infographic illustrating liver disease progression and clinical impact.

As a result, combining structured clinical data with direct imaging analysis may provide a more comprehensive and clinically realistic approach to liver cirrhosis assessment.

Preprocessing

Prior to model training, the dataset was preprocessed in MATLAB to improve data quality and ensure compatibility with machine learning algorithms. The preprocessing pipeline described below was consistently applied across all tabular machine learning models used in this project, including the Decision Tree, Random Forest, and MLP neural network models. The CNN imaging pipeline used separate image-specific preprocessing procedures.

  • Removed rows containing missing target classification labels
  • Retained only cirrhosis stages 1–3 for classification
  • Removed non-predictive variables including Stage, ID, N_Days, Drug, and Status
  • Converted categorical variables into numerical format using one-hot encoding
  • Handled missing numerical values through interpolation and mean-value replacement
  • Assigned missing categorical values to a separate category before encoding
  • Partitioned the dataset into 70% training, 15% validation, and 15% testing subsets
  • Applied z-score normalization using training set statistics to prevent data leakage

Ultrasound Imaging Dataset and CNN Preprocessing

Ultrasound Imaging Dataset

In addition to the structured clinical dataset, an image-based dataset was explored for liver fibrosis stage classification using ultrasound imaging data. The dataset consisted of approximately 6,323 grayscale ultrasound images organized into five classification categories, f0 through f4, representing increasing stages of liver fibrosis progression from no fibrosis to severe fibrosis.

The dataset was derived from the work of Y. Joo et al., titled “Classification of Liver Fibrosis From Heterogeneous Ultrasound Image”, published in IEEE Access in 2023. The ultrasound images were collected from Seoul St. Mary’s Hospital and used for CNN training and validation purposes.

Example adjacent ultrasound images used in the CNN-based liver fibrosis classification workflow.

CNN Image Preprocessing

Prior to CNN training, the ultrasound images were preprocessed in MATLAB to create a standardized dataset suitable for convolutional neural network classification.

The preprocessing pipeline described below was consistently applied across the CNN image dataset prior to architecture development and model training.

  • Obtained the dimensions of the first image to define a target image size
  • Resized all grayscale ultrasound images to 480 × 640 × 1
  • Preserved original images by saving resized copies into a separate dataset directory
  • Maintained image aspect ratios during resizing
  • Applied center zero-padding to preserve target dimensions
  • Iterated through fibrosis stage subfolders, f0 through f4, during processing
  • Created balanced training subsets using 400 images from each fibrosis stage category
  • Organized resized images into stage-specific class folders for CNN training

The balanced dataset approach was used to reduce training bias caused by unequal numbers of images between fibrosis stage categories. The resulting standardized dataset was then used for CNN architecture development and training.

Machine Learning Methods

Multiple machine learning approaches were explored throughout this project in order to compare model interpretability, ensemble learning performance, nonlinear classification capability, and image-based medical analysis for liver cirrhosis staging.

Decision Tree

The Decision Tree model was selected as the baseline classifier because it was the simplest and most interpretable machine learning approach explored in this project. Tree depth was limited during training to reduce overfitting while maintaining interpretability of the classification process.

The model provided a transparent framework for evaluating how individual clinical features contributed to liver cirrhosis stage classification and served as a comparison point for the more advanced ensemble and neural network models explored later in the project.

Generalized Decision Tree classification structure used as the baseline interpretable machine learning model.

Random Forest

Built-In MATLAB Implementation

The Random Forest model was implemented using MATLAB’s built-in fitcensemble function with bootstrap aggregation (bagging). Multiple decision trees were trained using randomized subsets of both the training data and feature space in order to improve generalization performance and reduce correlation between trees.

Predictions from individual trees were combined using majority voting to produce the final classification output, while feature importance analysis was used to identify the most influential clinical variables associated with liver cirrhosis staging.

From-Scratch Implementation

A second Random Forest implementation was developed from scratch in order to better understand the internal mechanics of ensemble learning algorithms. The custom implementation utilized bootstrap sampling, recursive decision tree construction, randomized feature selection, and majority voting aggregation across independently trained trees.

Although computationally slower than MATLAB’s built-in implementation, the from-scratch model provided greater insight into how Random Forest classifiers operate internally and how ensemble learning improves classification performance.

Generalized Random Forest ensemble architecture utilizing multiple independently trained Decision Trees.

MLP Neural Network

A custom multilayer perceptron (MLP) neural network was manually implemented in MATLAB for multiclass liver cirrhosis stage classification. Rather than relying on MATLAB’s built-in neural network training tools, the network was developed manually in order to provide direct control over network architecture, forward propagation, and backpropagation implementation.

The final network architecture consisted of an input layer, two hidden layers containing 64 and 32 neurons respectively, and an output layer containing three neurons corresponding to cirrhosis stages 1–3. ReLU activation functions were applied to the hidden layers, while the output layer utilized softmax activation for multiclass probability prediction.

The network was trained using gradient descent optimization with cross-entropy loss. During training, forward propagation was used to compute class probabilities, while backpropagation was manually implemented using the chain rule to calculate gradients and iteratively update network parameters.

Generalized Multilayer Perceptron neural network architecture used for nonlinear multiclass liver cirrhosis classification.

CNN Model

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) were explored for direct ultrasound image-based liver fibrosis classification. Unlike the tabular machine learning approaches used earlier in the project, CNNs operate directly on imaging data and are capable of automatically extracting hierarchical spatial features using convolutional filters and pooling operations.

The CNN architecture was developed in MATLAB and accepted grayscale ultrasound images with dimensions of 480 × 640 × 1. The network consisted of four convolutional blocks utilizing convolutional layers, batch normalization, ReLU activation functions, and max pooling operations for progressive feature extraction.

The number of learned filters increased throughout the network from 8 to 16, 32, and 64 filters in order to capture increasingly complex imaging features associated with liver fibrosis progression.

The final classification stage utilized a fully connected layer, softmax layer, and classification layer to assign ultrasound images into one of five fibrosis categories, f0 through f4.

The network was trained using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001, a mini-batch size of 32, and a maximum of 20 training epochs. Validation monitoring and early stopping criteria were also implemented during training to reduce overfitting and preserve the best-performing validation network.

Decision Tree Results

The Decision Tree model achieved classification accuracies ranging from approximately 70–74% when predicting liver cirrhosis stage. Analysis of the confusion matrix showed that Stage 2 was the most difficult classification category because many clinical indicators overlap with neighboring disease stages. In contrast, the model demonstrated stronger performance when identifying Stage 1 and Stage 3 cases, where clinical distinctions were more pronounced. Overall, the Decision Tree provided a simple and interpretable baseline for comparison against the more advanced ensemble and neural network models explored later in the project.

MLP Neural Network Results

The custom multilayer perceptron (MLP) neural network achieved a validation accuracy of approximately 87.33% and a testing accuracy of 87.76% when classifying liver cirrhosis stages 1–3. The relatively small difference between validation and testing performance suggests that the network generalized reasonably well to unseen data without severe overfitting.

MLP Confusion Matrix

MLP Training Loss

The training loss curve demonstrated stable convergence throughout the training process, with loss steadily decreasing across the 35,000 training epochs. Confusion matrix analysis also showed strong overall classification performance, with most predictions concentrated along the diagonal of the matrix. Similar to the other models explored in this project, the majority of misclassifications occurred between neighboring disease stages due to overlapping clinical indicators between adjacent stages of cirrhosis progression.

Although the MLP achieved strong classification performance, the improvement relative to the Random Forest model remained modest. This suggests that limitations in dataset size, feature separability, and overlap between clinical stages may have constrained the maximum achievable classification accuracy more than the specific model architecture itself.

CNN Image Classification Results

The convolutional neural network classified liver fibrosis ultrasound images into stages f0–f4. Additional training data and architecture refinement improved CNN accuracy from early 70–75% results to approximately 80% testing accuracy.

Conclusion and Future Work

This project explored multiple machine learning approaches for liver cirrhosis stage classification using both structured clinical datasets and medical imaging data. Decision Tree, Random Forest, Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), and preliminary CNN-based approaches were evaluated using publicly available liver cirrhosis datasets in MATLAB.

Among the tabular models, Random Forest achieved the highest overall performance with accuracies approaching 89%, while the custom MLP neural network achieved comparable performance near 88%. The relatively small performance gap between the Random Forest and MLP models suggests that limitations in dataset size, feature separability, and the complexity of nonlinear relationships within the structured clinical data may have reduced the advantages typically associated with deeper neural network architectures.

Feature analysis showed that biomarkers such as bilirubin, albumin, platelets, and prothrombin were among the most important predictors of cirrhosis severity.

The CNN-based image classification pipeline achieved promising preliminary results and demonstrated the broader potential of combining structured clinical records with medical imaging analysis for liver disease assessment.

Future work may include larger and more diverse datasets, improved neural network optimization techniques, deeper CNN architectures, transfer learning approaches, and multimodal systems capable of simultaneously analyzing clinical records and medical imaging data.
